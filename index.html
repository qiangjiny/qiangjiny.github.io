About

Qiang Ji is a researcher in artificial intelligence, machine learning, and computer vision. His current research focuses on uncertainty quantification and Bayesian/evidential deep learning, causal representation learning and domain generalization, knowledge-augmented learning, and human-centric visual intelligence.

His long-standing work in computer vision includes nonverbal human behavior analysis, facial behavior and gaze analysis, multimodal human understanding, three-dimensional face and human reconstruction, and probabilistic graphical models. He previously served as a Program Director at the U.S. National Science Foundation, managing programs in machine learning and computer vision.

Current Research

Uncertainty-Aware Learning

Bayesian and evidential deep learning, epistemic uncertainty quantification, uncertainty attribution, calibration, and open-world recognition.

Causal Learning

Causal representation learning, causal structure discovery, domain generalization, and stable representations beyond spurious correlations.

Knowledge-Augmented Learning

Integrating structural, symbolic, physical, and domain knowledge with deep learning to improve data efficiency, robustness, and generalization.

Emerging Direction: Multimodal Human-Centric Embodied AI

Our emerging work integrates nonverbal human behavior analysis and multimodal fusion with uncertainty-aware, causal, and knowledge-augmented learning toward multimodal human-centric embodied AI for social and assistive robots.
